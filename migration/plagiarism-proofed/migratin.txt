Migration is a major driver of population change, mostly influenced by socio-economic factors. As a demographic process, it brings noticeable changes to society by affecting not only the size of the population but also its structure and social characteristics. Besides migration, population dynamics are also shaped by biological factors like fertility and mortality. The impacts of population movement are felt in both origin and destination areas, as economic differences often force people to move. However, understanding the exact reasons why individuals choose a specific place to move can sometimes be quite complex.   As defined by the United Nations (1973), migration involves spatial mobility where individuals shift their usual residence from a departure point or place of origin to a new destination. As KC (2003) points out, internal migration involves people shifting their usual residence to a clear destination. A migrant is generally someone who moves away from their birthplace or changes their home on a seasonal, temporary, or semi-permanent basis, depending on how long they stay and why they moved.   According to the United Nations Population Fund (1993), migration serves as an indicator of changing social, economic, and political conditions at both national and international levels. It generally reflects deep economic and social gaps between places of origin and destination, which are often made easier by improved and affordable transport and communication. Additionally, population movement can be unpredictable due to short-term or long-term crises. While these emergencies usually trigger local movements first, people may still cross national borders when seeking safety.   From a classical economic perspective, migration helps balance population distribution with the availability of resources, as people naturally move from resource-poor areas to places with better opportunities. Generally, migration is categorized into internal and international movement. Internal migration refers to movement within national boundaries (in-migration and out-migration), whereas international migration involves relocating from one country to another (immigration and emigration).   Because it connects population growth to resource use, migration is a key topic in environmental and economic development studies. People may move between villages, across districts, or across national borders, primarily driven by economic reasons. Migration patterns also vary by age and gender.
